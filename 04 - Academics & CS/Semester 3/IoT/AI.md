### **Lecture 3: Core Technologies - Artificial Intelligence (AI)**

---

### **1. What is Artificial Intelligence (AI)?**

*   **Definition:** The study of computer systems designed to **model and apply human intelligence**.
*   **Core Idea:** It involves simulating **intelligent behavior** in machines.
*   **Simple Explanation:** The **capability of a machine to imitate intelligent human behavior** (e.g., learning, problem-solving).

---

### **2. Types of Artificial Intelligence**

*   **Weak AI (Narrow AI):**
    *   **Focus:** Designed and trained for **one specific task**.
    *   **Limitation:** Cannot perform beyond its programmed limitations.
    *   **Example:** Voice assistants (Siri, Alexa), recommendation algorithms, chess-playing computers.
    *   **Status:** **Common** in our daily lives.

*   **Strong AI (Artificial General Intelligence - AGI):**
    *   **Focus:** Can **understand, learn, and apply knowledge** across a wide range of tasks at a human level.
    *   **Status:** A goal that **researchers are striving to reach**. Not yet achieved.

*   **Super AI (Artificial Superintelligence - ASI):**
    *   **Focus:** **Surpasses human intelligence** and can perform any task better than a human.
    *   **Status:** Purely **hypothetical / a concept** for now.

---

### **3. Relationship: AI, Machine Learning (ML), Deep Learning (DL)**

This is a nested relationship, often visualized as concentric circles.

*   **Artificial Intelligence (AI):** The **broadest concept**. Any technique that enables a machine to mimic human behavior.
*   **Machine Learning (ML):** A **subset of AI**. A concrete approach to achieve AI.
    *   **Goal:** Enable systems to **learn automatically from experience/data** without explicit programming.
    *   **How:** Uses algorithms (e.g., decision trees, neural networks) to find patterns in data.
*   **Deep Learning (DL):** A **subset of ML**. A specific, powerful technique.
    *   **How:** Uses complex **neural networks** (inspired by the human brain) to learn from massive amounts of data.
    *   **Key Feature:** Can learn from **unstructured or unlabeled data** (e.g., images, text).
    *   **Requires:** Very **large datasets** and significant computing power.

**Summary:**
**AI** > **ML** > **DL**
(Deep Learning is a type of Machine Learning, which is a way to achieve Artificial Intelligence)

---

### **4. Applications of AI (Across Industries)**

*   **Healthcare:** Medical imaging analysis, drug discovery, personalized treatment plans, AI-powered chatbots for patient triage.
*   **Human Resources (HR):** Résumé screening, candidate sourcing, predicting employee attrition.
*   **Banking & Finance:** Fraud detection, algorithmic trading, credit scoring, customer service chatbots.
*   **Supply Chain:** Demand forecasting, inventory management, autonomous logistics and delivery.
*   **Daily Life:** Navigation apps, social media feeds, smart home devices.

---

### **5. Why is AI Booming Now?**

*   **Increased Computing Power:** Availability of powerful GPUs and cloud computing.
*   **Big Data:** Massive amounts of data available to train algorithms.
*   **Improved Algorithms:** Advancements in ML and DL models.
*   **Industry Adoption:** Widespread investment and integration into products and services.
*   **Technological Convergence:** Combination of AI with other technologies like IoT.

---

### **6. Advantages of AI**

1.  **Reduction in Human Error**
2.  **Zero Risks** (in dangerous environments, e.g., space exploration, bomb disposal)
3.  **24x7 Availability**
4.  **Digital Assistance** (e.g., chatbots, voice assistants)
5.  **New Inventions** (powering R&D in various fields)
6.  **Unbiased Decisions** (potentially, if trained on unbiased data)
7.  **Performs Repetitive Jobs** (freeing humans for creative tasks)
8.  **Faster Decision-making**
9.  **Pattern Identification** (in large, complex datasets)
10. **Medical Applications** (e.g., early disease detection)

---

### **7. Disadvantages of AI**

1.  **High Dependency on Data** (quality and quantity)
2.  **High Costs** (of development, implementation, and maintenance)
3.  **No Creativity** (lacks true innovation and out-of-the-box thinking)
4.  **Unemployment** (displacement of jobs involving repetitive tasks)
5.  **Make Humans Lazy** (over-reliance on automation)
6.  **No Ethics** (ethics must be programmed by humans)
7.  **Emotionless** (cannot understand human emotions or provide true empathy)
8.  **Bias can be planted in AI** (algorithms can perpetuate and amplify biases present in training data)
9.  **Potential for Misalignment** (AI may act in ways not intended by its creators)

---

### **8. Impact on Jobs (2023-2030)**

*   **Job Displacement:** AI is expected to replace **~85 million jobs** by 2025 (WEF estimate), especially in repetitive, manual, or data-processing roles (e.g., **65% of retail jobs**).
*   **Job Creation:** While automating routine tasks, AI will **create new jobs** in areas like AI development, data science, ethics, and maintenance.
*   **Shift in Skills:** The focus for humans will shift towards **creative, strategic, and high-thinking tasks** that are difficult for AI to perform, emphasizing skills like critical thinking, problem-solving, and empathy.

### **Exam Summary Focus:**

1.  **Define AI** and its three types (**Weak, Strong, Super**).
2.  Clearly explain the **relationship between AI, ML, and DL** (the nested hierarchy).
3.  List **3 advantages and 3 disadvantages** of AI.
4.  Understand the **dual impact of AI on jobs** (displacement vs. creation).
